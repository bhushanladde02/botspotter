What is BotSpotter?

Every day, thousands of AI bots crawl the internet. Some are collecting pages to train the next big model. Some are indexing the web for AI search answers. Some are fetching a page right now because a person just asked ChatGPT or Claude a question. Most websites never see any of it.

BotSpotter makes it visible. A network of volunteer websites and honeypots reports every bot visit here. We count them live, put them on the map, and give each bot a name that stays the same wherever it shows up — so Copper Heron #0042 is the same bot on your blog, on a news site, and on a server in Singapore. When a bot nobody has seen before appears on three independent sites, it gets discovered and named here first.

Three kinds of AI bot

  • Training crawlers — bulk-read the web to train models (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Bytespider, Common Crawl).
  • AI search — index pages so an AI can answer with citations (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot).
  • AI agents — fetch a page on behalf of a live user, this second (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User).

Plus the unidentified ones: automation that hasn't announced itself. Those are the interesting ones. They get names too.

Why it matters

  • If you run a website: find out which AI companies are reading your pages and how often — free, two-minute install, no code for Nginx/Apache. Add your site as a sensor and climb the leaderboard.
  • If you're curious: watch the AI economy happen in real time. Which countries are crawled most? Whose bots are busiest? What showed up this week that didn't exist last week?
  • If you write or research: a public, live, citable source on AI crawler activity that isn't owned by a single cloud provider.

Why do the bots have faces?

Every bot gets a robot avatar drawn from its fingerprint — the colour comes from its name (a Copper anything is copper), the face from the hash. No stock images, no AI art, nothing licensed from anyone: it's a few hundred lines of SVG, so the same bot has the same face forever, everywhere.

Run a bot? Claim it

Operators can claim their bot: prove control (make the bot fetch a one-time URL, or publish a token on its homepage), then give it a proper name, description and category. Claimed bots carry a ✓ and the generated name stays as an alias.

What we don't do

We don't store IP addresses or page contents, and we set no cookies. Human visits to this portal are recorded pseudonymously (a one-way hash, country/city, browser family, referring domain) so the owner can see how many real people come here and from where; see the privacy section. Bots are named by a hash, never exposed. The numbers here are visits seen by participating sites, not “the whole internet” — the more sensors join, the closer it gets.

5. Data: what the sensor sends and what we keep

Sent by the sensor, per request your server judges to be automated: the names (not values) of the request headers in order; the User-Agent string; the client IP address; the CF-IPCountry header if present; HTTP method and status code; the timestamp; and a flag for whether the path was robots.txt. Requests that look like ordinary browsers are filtered on your server and not sent. No page content, cookies, header values, query strings, form data, or other paths are sent.

Processed on arrival and then discarded: the IP address. We use it in memory to derive (a) a country and approximate region, and (b) for unidentified bots only, a coarse network prefix (/16, up to 65,536 addresses) that becomes part of the bot's fingerprint hash. The address itself is never written to disk, logs, or backups.

Stored: timestamp, your site id, the bot's fingerprint hash and derived name, a version-stripped User-Agent family, bot operator and category, country, region, a coarse geohash, and the robots.txt flag. Raw events are kept 90 days; hourly aggregates indefinitely.

Human visitors to this portal: we do not store your IP address and we set no cookie or browser identifier. Each page view is recorded as a pseudonymous visit: a salted hash of your IP and browser (the hash cannot be turned back into either; the salt is a server secret), the country and, when our CDN supplies it, the city; the page viewed; the referring domain (e.g. linkedin.com, “direct”) — never the referring page; your browser family, operating system, device class and preferred language. These rows are kept for up to three years, are visible only to the site owner, are used solely to understand and demonstrate the site’s audience (how many people, from where, how often they return), and are never sold or shared individually. Public pages show aggregates only. A daily-rotating hash additionally powers the public “humans today / this week” counters. We may also use Cloudflare Web Analytics, a cookie-free, privacy-preserving service of our CDN, for the same purpose.

Legal basis (where GDPR/UK GDPR applies): legitimate interest in measuring automated traffic and in understanding the portal’s own audience. The IP address is processed transiently for seconds and never retained; what is retained is pseudonymous and coarse by design. If you believe a stored record identifies you, email us and we will remove it.
